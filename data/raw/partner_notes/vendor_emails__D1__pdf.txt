I don’t have any idea what kind of facilities you have but maybe this is not that difficult a
problem.  Most nebulizers, or atomizers, produce around 3 to 10 µm drops. The final size
is controlled by how much volatile solvent is in the solution atomized and so eventually in
the drop, eg water is the most common.  Typically, you atomize an aerosol then dry it
(remove the solvent, water in this case) and are left with the non volatile species – this is
standard aerosol technology.  If you have no solvent that evaporates you have the original
drops from the atomizer, so the size range is somewhat set. Not sure how viscosity affects
size of atomized drops, could be more related to surface tension.   Drops larger than 10
µm fall out quickly and so hard to use effectively.
 
As for deposition on the part of interest, I would just put a tracer in the solution going
through the atomizer and detect it on the part of interest.  Eg, a fluorescent tracer that
would be readily visible with a black light or light of specific wavelength. Need to find a
compatible tracer for the solvent used (ie, the liquid in the atomizer along with your
disinfectant, or whatever it is).
 
Check out fluoresceine, it is soluble in water and commonly used for these types of
applications.
