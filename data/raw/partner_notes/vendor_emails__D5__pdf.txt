Good afternoon Andrew and Jim,
I reviewed the NDA with my counsel, and there are only two clarifications they require before I
can sign. These aren’t changes to the business terms — just standard IP protection language for
founders who are bringing an existing patent-pending product to a manufacturer for evaluation.
While we are coming to Spray for assistance with the formulation, we’re still not entering this as a
typical vendor seeking standard filling or contract manufacturing services. We are approaching
this as a company with a patent-pending invention that includes proprietary mechanisms,
geometry, and product functionality already in development. In these situations, industry
standards handle IP differently because the inventor must retain rights to their pre-existing and
independently developed technology.
At the moment, Section 4 includes language stating that:
“any protocol, software, algorithm, technique and/or methodology developed during its
association with Spray shall be the property of Spray,”
the other party “shall not file any application for seeking intellectual property rights,” and
the other party “shall not oppose” any intellectual property application filed by Spray.
This wording is not acceptable for companies presenting patented or patent-pending technology,
because it can unintentionally transfer ownership of the inventor’s IP or restrict their ability to
protect improvements. These provisions are appropriate for vendors or contractors working f o r 
Spray, but not for an external company sharing its own proprietary invention w i t h  Spray for
evaluation and potential collaboration.
To avoid that misunderstanding, my counsel requires two standard clarifications:
1. Each party retains ownership of their pre-existing and independently-developed
intellectual property.
2. Any jointly developed work would require a separate written agreement outside of the
NDA.
These points are industry-standard whenever a company shares a patent-pending concept with a
potential manufacturing or formulation partner. They don’t expand our rights or limit Spray’s
rights in any way — they simply ensure that the NDA operates strictly as a confidentiality
agreement, not an unintended IP assignment.
