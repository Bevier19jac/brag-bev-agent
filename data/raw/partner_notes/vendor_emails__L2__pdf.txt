Good afternoon Andrew and Jim,
I reviewed the NDA with my counsel, and there are only two clarifications they require
before I can sign. These aren’t changes to the business terms — just standard IP
protection language for founders who are bringing an existing patent-pending
product to a manufacturer for evaluation.
While we are coming to Spray for assistance with the formulation, we’re still not
entering this as a typical vendor seeking standard filling or contract manufacturing
services. We are approaching this as a company with a patent-pending invention
that includes proprietary mechanisms, geometry, and product functionality already in
development. In these situations, industry standards handle IP differently because the
inventor must retain rights to their pre-existing and independently developed
technology.
At the moment, Section 4 includes language stating that:
“any protocol, software, algorithm, technique and/or methodology developed
during its association with Spray shall be the property of Spray,”
the other party “shall not file any application for seeking intellectual property
rights,” and
the other party “shall not oppose” any intellectual property application filed by
Spray.
3/7/26, 11:22 AM iCloud Mail
https://www.icloud.com/mail/ 7/21

This wording is not acceptable for companies presenting patented or patent-pending
technology, because it can unintentionally transfer ownership of the inventor’s IP or
restrict their ability to protect improvements. These provisions are appropriate for
vendors or contractors working for Spray, but not for an external company sharing its
own proprietary invention with Spray for evaluation and potential collaboration.
To avoid that misunderstanding, my counsel requires two standard clarifications:
1. Each party retains ownership of their pre-existing and independently-
developed intellectual property.
2. Any jointly developed work would require a separate written agreement
outside of the NDA.
These points are industry-standard whenever a company shares a patent-pending
concept with a potential manufacturing or formulation partner. They don’t expand our
rights or limit Spray’s rights in any way — they simply ensure that the NDA operates
strictly as a confidentiality agreement, not an unintended IP assignment.
I would also like the NDA to explicitly state that this version supersedes any previous
version circulated or discussed, to ensure both parties are aligned on the final
governing document.
I hope these terms are acceptable and something you can work with. We’re excited to
move forward and want to keep everything clean and clear on both sides, so this is
really just a legal housekeeping step. I’m happy to sign immediately once those
clarifications are included.
