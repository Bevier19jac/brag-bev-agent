Hi Andrew and Jim,
I reviewed the NDA with my counsel, and there are only two clarifications they require before I can sign.
These aren’t changes to the business terms — just standard IP protection language for founders who are
bringing an existing patent-pending product to a manufacturer for evaluation.
As you know, we’re not coming to Spray as a typical vendor seeking filling or formulation services. We
are approaching this as a company with a patent-pending invention that needs to be evaluated for
potential partnership and manufacturing. In these situations, industry standards handle IP differently
because the inventor must retain rights to their pre-existing and independently developed technology.
At the moment, Section 4 includes language stating that:
“any protocol, software, algorithm, technique and/or methodology developed during its association
with Spray shall be the property of Spray,” and
the other party “shall not file any application for seeking intellectual property rights,” and
the other party “shall not oppose” any intellectual property application filed by Spray.
This wording is not acceptable for companies presenting patented or patent-pending technology, because
it can unintentionally transfer ownership of the inventor’s IP or restrict their ability to protect
improvements. These provisions are designed for vendors or contractors working for Spray — not for an
external company sharing its own proprietary invention with Spray.
To avoid that misunderstanding, my counsel requires two standard clarifications:
1. Each party retains ownership of their pre-existing and independently-developed intellectual
property.
2. Any jointly developed work would require a separate written agreement outside of the NDA.
