## Section 2: Page 2
Testing Overview
• Purpose of testing was to determine the spray coverage of 
the product inside a 96-gallon trash bin.
• 4 height levels in the trash bin were selected, including the 
interior lid surface 
• Mounted 16 1”x1” paper filter coupons (4 per height 
including 4 on interior lid surface) to 96-gallon bin interior 
walls using adhesive.
• Initial mass of the spray can, and test coupons were 
recorded.
• Placed aerosol can upright at the center of the bin floor 
using double sided tape to secure it.
• Installed internal lighting and video recording device.
• Actuated can for full discharge with the lid closed.

## Section 3: Page 3
Testing Overview
• Allowed foam to settle for 3 minutes.
• Post-testing mass of the coupons was taken, and the 
surface deposition was calculated. 
• Removed coupons and placed them in labeled containers.
• Post-testing mass of the spray can was recorded.

## Section 20: Page 20
Trial 1&2 Summary
• The 2nd spray can had more output than the can used in the 
1st attempt.
• The can sprayed high enough to reach the interior lid 
surface.
• The contents inside the can had a nice peppermint smell.
• The fan shaped spray covered half the trash can.
• Since the can sprays in a fan shape 5 of the 16 coupons 
were the only ones to get wet. 1 at the 1ft level, 1 at the 2ft 
level, 2 at the 3ft level, and 1 at the lid.

## Section 21: Page 21
Trial 3-5 Summary
• The three new samples with the updated nozzle was tested.
• The new samples that had the nozzle modified sprayed higher than the 
two initial spray cans that were tested.
• The can sprayed high enough to reach the interior lid surface.
• The contents inside the can had a nice peppermint smell.
• All tested cans discharged primarily as a single stream through most of 
can life and transitioned to the intended full-cone only near depletion 
(~final 10% remaining).
• Since the cans spray in an angle towards the latch when actuated, they 
covered half the trash can.

## Section 22: Page 22
Discussion
• We noticed the spray mostly shoots in a constant stream until it 
gets close to empty (around 10% left inside) and then it starts 
spraying in a full cone shape as intended.
• Spray plume was biased in a consistent direction due to the 
latch on the nozzle being angled when the spray can is 
activated, leading to preferential coverage of one side of the 
bin.
• This is likely due to high actuation pressure which directly 
creates the two failure modes observed.
• This can be fixed with valve selection (lower spring rate), 
smoother latch mechanics, and reduced friction so the user 
doesn’t need to push as hard.
