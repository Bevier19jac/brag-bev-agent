Purpose: To characterize vertical spray reach and internal surface coverage of a single -use cleanser 
spray when deployed inside a closed 96 -gallon residential trash bin using gravimetric mass -gain 
deposition coupons (mg/in²). 
 
Background: In enclosed volumes, internal deposition is governed by spray plume geometry, 
dispersion, wall impaction, gravitational settling, and post -deposition migration (dripping/pooling). 
Products marketed as “foaming” may exhibit foam-rise effects; however, when discharge behavior is 
spray-dominant, coverage is primarily a function of spray transport and settling dynamics. 
 
Methods: Five aerosol cans were evaluated using one full, continuous upright discharge per can (five 
independent trials). Deposition was quantified using 1 in² absorbent filter paper coupons installed at 
four locations: 1 ft, 2 ft, and 3 ft above the bin bottom on the interior wall and the interior lid surface. 
Four coupons were installed at each location (16 coupons per trial). Testing was performed with the 
bin lid closed during discharge and a 3 -minute dwell period at controlled conditions of 70°F and 60 % 
RH. Coupons were pre-weighed and post-weighed on an analytical balance and deposition density was 
reported as mg/in². Internal lighting and video documentation were used to support qualitative 
interpretation. 
 
Results: Trial 1 was impacted by an unexpected fan-shaped spray and can tip-over, resulting in highly 
directional impingement, incomplete measurements, and only one coupon showing measurable 
deposition (106 mg/in²). Trial 2 produced a complete dataset (n = 16), with mean deposition of 12.00 
mg/in² (1 ft), 5.75 mg/in² (2 ft), 156.75 mg/in² (3 ft), and 23.00 mg/in² (lid), characterized by non -
uniform deposition with multiple 0 mg/in² coupons and isolated high-mass events. Trials 3 and 4, using 
modified nozzles, discharged primarily as a coherent stream with a consistent directional bias, yielding 
substantially lower deposition (Trial 3: 5.75, 0.75, 8.50, 0.50 mg/in²; Trial 4: 0.75, 0.50, 0.75, 0.75 
mg/in² at 1 ft, 2 ft, 3 ft, and lid, respectively). Trial 5 produced moderate deposition (11.25, 1.75, 10.00, 
and 26.00 mg/in²), with high variability at the 3 ft and lid locations (±1 SD up to 50.02 mg/in²), 
indicating localized impaction rather than uniform coverage. 
 
Conclusion: Across the completed datasets, the cleanser spray deposited measurable mass on 
elevated internal surfaces; however, deposition was highly variable and strongly influenced by 
discharge behavior and plume geometry. Trial 2 showed substantial deposition at th e 3 ft wall and 
measurable lid deposition, occurring as localized impaction rather than uniform coverage. Trials 3 and 
4 exhibited stream -like discharge behavior, resulting in minimal deposition at all locations and 
preferential wetting of one side of the bin. Trial 5 produced moderate deposition at 1 ft and 3 ft with 
the highest mean deposition on the lid, but remained highly non -uniform. Trial 1 is retained for 
traceability only due to documented deviations. 
 
 
Study Overview 
The objective of this non -GLP screening study was to 
quantify internal coverage efficiency and vertical reach of a 
single-use cleanser spray in a closed 96 -gallon bin using 
area-normalized gravimetric deposition coupons. Five 
independent full -discharge tri als were planned and 
executed as one discharge per can. Deposition was 
assessed at four locations spanning the internal vertical 
profile and lid interior using sixteen 1 in² coupons per trial 
to generate an engineering -relevant deposition profile for 
enclosed-bin application performance. 
 
 
Background 
Closed-bin deposition is driven by the interaction of spray 
plume geometry and momentum with the enclosure 
boundaries and by subsequent transport mechanisms, 
including gravitational settling, turbulent dispersion, 
impaction, and post -deposition migration v ia dripping and 
pooling. These mechanisms can produce strong spatial 
heterogeneity even when total discharged mass is high. 
When product behavior is spray -dominant rather than 
foam-dominant, “vertical reach” is best interpreted as the 
ability of the spray plume and entrained droplets to reach 
elevated surfaces (upper walls and lid) rather than foam -
rise height.

Test Configuration 
 
Testing was performed in a closed 96 -gallon residential 
trash bin configured to represent enclosed, in -use 
deployment conditions. The spray can was positioned 
upright at the center of the bin floor and actuated as a 
single continuous full discharge while t he lid remained 
closed. Deposition was measured using 1 in × 1 in (1.0 in²) 
absorbent filter paper coupons mounted flush to the 
interior surfaces at four locations: 1 ft, 2 ft, and 3 ft above 
the bin bottom along the interior wall and on the interior lid 
surface. Four coupons were installed per location (16 total 
per trial) with wall coupons distributed circumferentially 
and lid coupons distributed across representative lid 
regions. The bin was equipped with internal LED 
illumination and an internally mounted, sealed smartphone 
camera for video documentation. Testing was conducted 
under controlled laboratory conditions of 70°F and 60% RH, 
and coupons were collected after a 3 -minute closed -lid 
dwell period for gravimetric determination of deposition 
density (mg/in²). 
 
Deviations / Trial 1 Deviation 
 
Trial 1 included a major deviation identified during 
execution. The nozzle/orifice configuration was different 
than expected and produced a fan -shaped spray pattern 
rather than a cone spray. This altered plume geometry 
increased directional impingement and reduced 
circumferential distribution, and several coupon post-mass 
measurements as well as the surface deposition were not 
recorded. As a result, Trial 1 is documented for traceability 
but is not used as the primary dataset for coverage 
efficiency interpretation. (To see data go to Table A1 in the 
appendix). 
 
Testing Conditions 
Testing was performed at ARE Labs under controlled 
environmental conditions of 70°F and 60% RH. A single 96 -
gallon residential trash bin was used as the test enclosure, 
and the lid remained closed during discharge and the post-
discharge dwell period. 
 
Samples Tested 
The test articles consisted of pressurized, single -use 
cleanser spray cans in which the nozzle locks itself once 
activated. The spray cans are designed to dispense a 
cleaning formulation as a spray plume. Each can was 
discharged in a single continuous actuation with the can 
maintained upright. During testing, the product exhibited 
spray-dominant behavior rather than sustained foam 
formation, and performance was therefore interpreted 
primarily in terms of spray transport, impaction, and 
settling within the enclosed bin.

Methods 
Each trial consisted of placing one aerosol can upright at the 
center of the bin floor, initiating internal video recording, 
and closing the bin lid. The can was discharged continuously 
to empty while the lid remained closed. After discharge, the 
bin remained closed for a 3 -minute dwell period to allow 
spray settling and deposition stabilization. Coupons were 
then removed sequentially with minimal disturbance, 
placed into labeled containers, and weighed. 
Deposition coupons were 1 in × 1 in absorbent filter paper 
(1.0 in²). Sixteen coupons were installed per trial, with four 
coupons at each location: 1 ft, 2 ft, and 3 ft on the interior 
wall and four coupons on the interior lid surface. Each 
coupon was uniquely labeled and pre -weighed prior to 
installation. Post -test mass was recorded, mass gain was 
calculated as post minus pre, and deposition density was 
reported directly as mg/in². 
Figure 4: Coupon placement and spray configuration inside

RESULTS: Trial 1 (Deviation / Partial Data) 
Trial 1 was impacted by a fan -shaped spray pattern 
associated with an unexpected nozzle/orifice configuration 
as well as the can tipping over . Several coupon post -test 
masses were not recorded, and only one coupon showed 
measurable deposition. The one recorded deposition event 
was 106 mg/in² (Coupon ID 1; 64 mg pre to 170 mg post). 
The can mass decreased from 131.51 g to 77.29 g, 
corresponding to an observed discharged mass of 54.22 g. 
Due to altered plume geometry and incomplete 
measurements, T rial 1 resul ts are considered potentially 
skewed and are not used for deposition profiling.

Deviations/Observations 
Additional Observations (Trials 3–5): Three new spray cans 
were supplied with a modified nozzle intended to produce 
a full cone spray. During testing, discharge behavior for 
Trials 3 –5 was predominantly a coherent stream for the 
majority of can life, with the intended dispersed spray 
behavior occurring primarily near end -of-can conditions 
(~10% remaining). The discharge trajectory was 
consistently biased in the same direction, resulting in 
preferential wetting of approximately one side of the bin 
interior. Subsequent evaluation identified that the 
actuation latch/trigger mechanism becomes angled after 
actuation and imposes an off -axis load on the 
actuator/nozzle during discharge, causing the spray plume 
to deflect toward the latch direction. 
Corrective Actions / Recommendations 
To mitigate the consistent off-axis plume bias observed in 
Trials 3–5, corrective actions should focus on eliminating 
latch-induced actuator tilt and increasing the sensitivity of 
the actuator/nozzle to side loading. Recommended actions 
include valve selection (lower spring rate), smoother latch 
mechanics, and reduced friction so the user doesn’t need to 
push as hard. Redesigning the latch/trigger to apply 
actuation force through the actuator centerline (or 
symmetrically), and adding positive actuator 
centering/guidance features (e.g., pilot/guide on the valve 
stem interface) to prevent the actuator from cocking under 
load. A fixtured actuation test that bypasses the latch is 
recommended to confirm restoration of coaxial plume 
alignment prior to additional coverage evaluations. 
 
Conclusions 
Across the completed datasets, the cleanser spray 
demonstrated the ability to deposit measurable mass on 
elevated interior surfaces, including the interior lid; 
however, deposition behavior was strongly influenced by 
discharge behavior and plume geometry d uring the single-
use actuation. When the discharge was characterized by a 
dispersed spray (Trial 2), substantial deposition was 
achieved at the upper wall (3 ft) and on the lid, confirming 
that vertical reach to elevated surfaces is achievable. This 
deposition, however, occurred as localized high -impaction 
events rather than uniform circumferential wetting. 
In contrast, Trials 3 and 4 exhibited discharge behavior 
dominated by a coherent stream during most of the 
actuation, resulting in substantially reduced deposition at 
all vertical locations and minimal lid coverage. Trial 5 
produced intermediate results, with moderate deposition 
at 1 ft and 3 ft and the highest mean deposition on the lid; 
however, large standard deviations at the 3 ft and lid 
locations indicate continued non-uniform deposition driven 
by isolated impaction and coalescence events. 
Overall, the results indicate that vertical deposition 
performance for this single -actuation fogger is highly 
sensitive to actuator alignment and plume stability during 
discharge. The consistent off -axis plume bias observed in 
Trials 3–5 was attributed to latch/trigger-induced actuator 
tilt, demonstrating that relatively small mechanical 
asymmetries during actuation can materially limit coverage 
uniformity. Trial 1 is retained for traceability only and is not 
considered representative due to nozzle deviatio n, can 
instability, and incomplete measurements.
